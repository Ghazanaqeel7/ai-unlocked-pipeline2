# 013-canva-ai-week-of-posts-long: script

Target 509s (~1416 words at 170 wpm). Markers drive the retention rules (pipeline/script/README.md).

[HOOK 0:00] Social posts take forever. You open Canva on Monday, and by Friday you have made three posts and hated two of them. In this video you will build a full week of branded posts in one sitting, using Canva's own AI and automation tools.

But first, the one setup step that makes every post after it faster. Skip it and the rest of this workflow falls apart. I will show you exactly where it lives.

[CHAPTER 1 0:27] Set up your Brand Kit

[REHOOK 0:27] Chapter one is your Brand Kit. A Brand Kit holds your colors, fonts and logos so every design starts on brand instead of blank.

Open Canva, go to Brand in the side panel, and choose Brand Kits. If you have more than one kit, pick the one you want to edit.

[REHOOK 0:46] Here is the part most people miss. Inside the Brand Kit there is a brand voice section. That is where you write down how your brand sounds.

Check your plan first. Canva says brand voice is available on Pro, Teams, Education and Nonprofits plans. And on a team, only owners, administrators and brand designers can add or edit it.

[REHOOK 1:07] Keep your brand voice guidelines short and concrete. Say who you write for, how formal you sound, and any words you always or never use. Then save.

Why bother? Once a brand voice exists, Canva says its guidelines also apply when you use the Magic Write shortcut, the forward slash key. You set the tone once and every caption inherits it.

[REHOOK 1:28] Here is a brief you can adapt. Ask for a friendly Instagram caption for a small bakery, about a weekend special, two sentences, upbeat tone. Swap in your own business, offer and tone, then regenerate until one line sounds like you.

Before you leave the Brand Kit, check that your colors, fonts and logo are in there too. That way every template you open next starts looking like you.

[CHAPTER 2 1:53] Write the week with Magic Write

[REHOOK 1:53] Chapter two is writing the week. Open a new Instagram post design, and pick a simple template that has room for a headline and a caption.

Select a text box. A floating toolbar appears, and it has a Magic Write button. You can also press the forward slash key and choose Magic Write.

[REHOOK 2:12] Magic Write works from a prompt. Tell it the topic, who it is for, and the tone you want. Canva's own guidance says you can include tone in your brief, from playful to formal.

Be specific about length too. Ask for two sentences, or a word limit, and Magic Write has a clear target instead of rambling.

[REHOOK 2:32] Treat the first result as a draft, not a finished post. Read it out loud, cut anything that does not sound like you, and keep the best line as your headline.

You can also save your own writing style. Select some text, open Magic Write, choose Change voice, and save a tone of voice you can pick again later.

[REHOOK 2:53] Now write the week. Plan seven angles, one for each day: a tip, a mistake, a before and after, a question, a story, a checklist and a recap. Run each through the same brief.

Do not publish raw AI text. Always fact check numbers and claims yourself, and read each caption once before it goes anywhere near a schedule.

[REHOOK 3:14] A good habit is keeping a swipe file of your best captions. When a draft lands well, paste it into your table as an example, and reuse its structure in the next brief.

That gives you seven drafts. Next we make the designs, and this is where Canva stops being a one at a time tool.

[CHAPTER 3 3:34] Turn drafts into designs

[REHOOK 3:34] Chapter three is Bulk Create. It takes data in rows and columns and links it to placeholder elements in your design, so one template becomes many posts.

First, build one finished post as your template. Give every piece of changing text its own text box, because Canva needs a separate box for each data field.

[REHOOK 3:54] Then build your data. Make a simple table with a column for the headline and a column for the caption, and one row per post. Seven days means seven rows.

A quick tip on columns. Start with just a headline and a caption. Once that works, add a column for a hashtag line or an image, and connect those too.

[REHOOK 4:15] You can upload a CSV or Excel file, or just enter the data by hand inside Canva. Entering it by hand is fine for a week of posts.

Connect the data to your design. Select an element, open More, and choose Connect data. Or click Auto-match fields and let Canva try to connect everything for you.

[REHOOK 4:35] Check the preview before you generate. Bulk Create makes one design for every row, so a typo in your table becomes seven typos in your designs.

A few limits to know. Bulk Create is desktop only, it is limited to certain plans, and Canva documents up to 300 rows in one batch. A week of posts fits easily.

[REHOOK 4:56] One more catch. Image links do not work in Bulk Create. If your posts use photos, upload them, or pick them from your Canva library.

Keep the table tidy. Short headlines fit a template better, and a long caption can overflow a text box, so check the longest row in the preview first.

[REHOOK 5:15] Click Create designs, and Canva generates one design for each row of data. So your seven rows become seven finished designs.

Now the same post often needs other shapes. Magic Resize adapts a design to another size, Canva's design school covers it in its social media lesson.

After a resize, always look at the result. Canva's guidance on reshaping a post says to keep your key text and faces clear of the protected space.

[REHOOK 5:42] Only resize the posts that need it. If a design already works in every shape you publish in, skip this step and save the time.

[CHAPTER 4 5:51] Schedule it all

Chapter four is scheduling. Canva has a built in Content Planner, so you can plan and schedule posts without leaving Canva.

From the homepage side menu choose More, then Content Planner. Select Add New, then Post, and pick the design you just made.

[REHOOK 6:06] Connect your social accounts. Canva lets you connect more than one account, and you choose who sees or can publish to each one.

If you manage posts for a team, set each account's visibility on purpose. A private account keeps scheduling to you, while team viewable lets everyone see what is going out.

[REHOOK 6:25] Canva's Content Planner page lists platforms including Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn, and its help center says more are on the way. Check the current list for your account.

A note on Instagram. Canva says scheduling to Instagram has to be done from a desktop browser. On mobile you can view the planner and schedule from the editor.

[REHOOK 6:45] Do a final pass on the calendar view. You want a mix of angles across the week, not three tips in a row, and every post on the day you meant.

Pick a day and a time for each post, then click Schedule. Spread the seven posts across the week instead of stacking them on one day.

[REHOOK 7:06] Plans change. Canva says you can pause and edit a scheduled post without uploading the file again, so a typo on Wednesday is a quick fix.

Once posts are live, Canva's Content Planner can show insights like impressions, clicks, likes and comments. Use them to decide which angle to repeat next week.

[REHOOK 7:24] Write down what worked. Next week, change one thing, the angle, the time or the hook, so you actually learn from the results instead of guessing.

Let us put the whole workflow together. Set your brand voice once. Write seven drafts with Magic Write. Turn them into designs with Bulk Create. Resize where you need to. Schedule the week.

[REHOOK 7:45] If you only have time for one upgrade today, set your brand voice. It takes a few minutes, and every caption you write afterward starts closer to done.

Your first run will feel slower than doing one post by hand. The second week is where it pays off, because the brand voice and the template are already done.
